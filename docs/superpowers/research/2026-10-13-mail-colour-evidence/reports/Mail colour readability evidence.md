# Let Nord decorate while shape signals

The evidence supports Nord as a gentle accent layer in Thunderbird 158 only where a non-colour cue already carries the meaning and the hue adds speed on top. For spotting unread mail, that means restoring a leading unread dot, which the GNOME theme currently hides so that unread cards differ by bold alone, and giving that dot a single Frost accent. For spotting the sender, it means position at the left of the row and weight, not colour. For reading, it means neutral quoted text with coloured bars, links that keep an underline, and signatures dimmed by an opacity tuned separately for each mode. The measurements explain why hue cannot carry more. Passed through the theme's OKLab lightness rule, every Nord accent clears 4.5:1 on every measured background (4.91:1 minimum in light mode, 7.47:1 in dark), but the rule pins all nine Frost and Aurora colours to one lightness, so nord9 and nord10 become a single colour (ΔE2000 3.0) and red against green falls to ΔE2000 2.4 to 5.6 for deuteranopes. What survives in both modes and under all three dichromacies is the cool-against-warm axis (Frost against nord12 or nord13, worst case ΔE2000 24.6 or more), which supports a budget of one cool accent, one warm accent, and nord11 kept for junk. New calculations against the theme's own colours add two limits: on a selected row in light mode, nord7, nord8 and nord14 drop to 4.44 to 4.45:1 as text, and in dark mode every adjusted accent sits only 1.34 to 1.36:1 from body text, so colour alone can never mark a link there. No controlled study tests any of these choices in a mail client; the case rests on vision science, accessibility criteria, product practice and measurement.

## A leading dot beats bold alone for spotting unread mail

Visual-search research gives a clear account of what makes one row jump out of a list, and it favours a mark over a weight change. Wolfe and Horowitz grade colour, motion, orientation and size as **"undoubted" guiding attributes** and luminance polarity as "probable"; font weight appears nowhere on their list ([Wolfe and Horowitz 2004](https://search.bwh.harvard.edu/new/pubs/WolfeHorowitzNRN04.pdf)). Guided Search 6.0 adds the condition that matters most for a mail list: guidance strengthens as the target differs more from distractors and as **distractors become more alike**, and it favours items near the point of fixation ([Wolfe 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8965574)). Luminance works as well as hue: search time stayed roughly constant with set size when the target-distractor luminance difference was large, "similar for targets defined by luminance and chromaticity" ([Nagy and Sanchez 1992](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872089203400507)). In crowd-sourced tests of text highlighting, font size worked best as a single technique and some pairs of techniques interfered with each other ([Strobelt et al. 2016](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20160101-guidelines-for-effective-usage-of-text-highlighting-techniques)). Put together, a marker present only on unread rows is the textbook unique feature among homogeneous distractors, while bold changes stroke width by a step that may sit below the large difference guidance needs. No study compares bold, dot and bar head to head in a list, so this ranking is an inference from the general findings.

Mature clients converge on the same answer. **No client surveyed relies on a colour change alone for unread.** Apple Mail marks unread with a blue dot and offers bold only as a column-layout option ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/mail/mlhlp1010); [Apple Viewing settings](https://support.apple.com/guide/mail/cpmlprefview)); new Outlook pairs a bold subject with a filled blue circle ([Dartmouth Services](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/Article/64577/Outlook-Icons)); Mimestream bolds senders and shows unread dates in blue ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2023/05/24/why-i-use-mimestream-for-gmail/)). Thunderbird's own card view pairs bold sender and subject with a green read-status dot, but its base style sets read senders at weight 600 and unread at bold, a narrow step ([threadCard.css](https://github.com/mozilla/releases-comm-central/blob/master/mail/themes/shared/mail/threadCard.css)). The weak spot of weight-only cues shows up in user complaints that Gmail's unread rows became "less bold" and hard to tell from read ones on Samsung phones, low-quality evidence but consistent with the theory ([Samsung Community](https://eu.community.samsung.com/t5/galaxy-s24-series/new-gmails-no-longer-in-bold-ui-7/td-p/12368286)).

The GNOME theme removes the strongest of Thunderbird's two channels. Its card rules set `.thread-card-column:first-child { display: none }`, which hides the read-status dot column, force sender, date and subject to normal weight in the view colour, and restore bold only on the sender and subject of unread rows ([thunderbird-gnome-theme mail.css](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/thunderbird-gnome-theme/blob/main/theme/parts/mail.css), read from the installed checkout). The resulting 400-to-700 step is wider than stock Thunderbird's 600-to-700, and bold satisfies WCAG 1.4.1 because it is not a colour cue ([W3C, Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)). It is still one channel. Restoring the dot adds a second channel whose presence is a shape difference, so it passes 1.4.1 whatever its hue, and it sits at the left edge beside the sender, where web eyetracking puts **80% of viewing time** on the left half of the page ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-leans-left/)).

The dot's colour has to pass contrast but should not be expected to carry much meaning. As a graphic that conveys state, it needs 3:1 against its neighbours under SC 1.4.11 ([W3C, Understanding 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Raw nord8 fails that on every light background measured (1.68 to 2.00:1), while the theme-adjusted nord8 gives **5.29:1 on the light message list and 9.44:1 on the dark one**, holding 4.45:1 and 6.85:1 on a selected row (computed here with the [WCAG 2.2 formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-contrast-ratio) against the theme's list background and its 10% selection tint from [light.css](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/thunderbird-gnome-theme/blob/main/theme/colors/light.css) and [dark.css](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/thunderbird-gnome-theme/blob/main/theme/colors/dark.css)). Hue at dot size is unreliable. At the CSS reference pixel, a 6 to 8 px dot spans 7.7 to 10.2 arcminutes, well inside the 18 to 25 arcminute central zone that lacks working blue-sensitive cones ([Williams et al. 1981](https://aria.cvs.rochester.edu/papers/williams-etal_VR1981.pdf); [Davies and Morland 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12770973/)), so a blue-against-grey judgement is exactly the one the eye makes worst at that size. The dot works by presence and lightness; its hue is decoration. APCA's guidance puts solid semantic icons at no less than 5.5 px in their smallest dimension ([APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). nord8 has the best claim to the dot: Nord's documentation calls it "the bright and shiny primary accent color" for elements that "require the most visual attention" ([Nord docs](https://www.nordtheme.com/docs/colors-and-palettes)), and the Thunderbird Nord Hardt theme uses it for the unread dot ([Nord Hardt colors.scss](https://github.com/artrz/thunderbird-nord-hardt-theme/blob/main/src/styles/colors.scss)). Other mail ports pick nord7 (aerc) or a bold nord13 (mu4e), so the precedent leaves the exact swatch to taste.

The dot keeps its pop-out only if the rest of the list stays quiet. Random hue variation degraded detection of size and density targets in Healey and Enns' experiments, which led them to the rule that secondary data must not mask what the viewer most needs ([Healey and Enns 2012](https://visionlab-psych.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2015/06/140_HealeyEnns_IEEE2011.pdf)); clutter makes it harder for any new item to draw attention ([Rosenholtz et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1167/7.2.17)); and NN/g's practical limit is two primary and two secondary colours, because "if everything is contrasted, then nothing stands out" ([NN/g, Visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)). Every coloured attachment icon, tag and star in the list makes the unread dot less unique.

## Sender recognition rides on position and weight, not hue

The second priority, seeing who sent a message, has a simpler answer than the first. Readers fixate the first two words of a line far more than the third ([NN/g, F-shaped pattern](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/)), "people simply don't open messages that don't have recognizable sender information", and many inbox views truncate the From field after **20 to 25 characters** ([NN/g, Transactional email](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/transactional-and-confirmation-email/)). Thunderbird's cards already put the sender's display name first on the first line ([Thunderbird blog](https://blog.thunderbird.net/2023/07/getting-started-with-the-main-window-of-thunderbird-115-supernova/)). The evidence therefore asks for the sender at the left, at full text contrast, wide enough that a name survives truncation, and bold when unread. All of this eyetracking comes from web pages and newsletters between 2006 and 2017, so its transfer to a dense mail list is plausible but undemonstrated.

Colouring the sender is the tempting move, and the measurements argue against it. Nord is a pastel palette, so an accent applied to text always has less luminance contrast than the neutral text it replaces: on the light list the theme's text reaches 11.67:1 while adjusted accents reach 5.28 to 5.75:1, and on the dark list white reaches 14.68:1 against 8.31 to 9.46:1 for the accents (computed with the [WCAG 2.2 formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-contrast-ratio)). Since hierarchy comes from "the contrast in value and saturation between the element and the context" rather than from the colour itself ([NN/g, Visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)), a coloured sender trades luminance salience for hue salience. Three further costs follow. In light mode a selected row pushes **nord7, nord8 and nord14 to 4.44 to 4.45:1, below the 4.5:1 text minimum** ([W3C, Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)), while nord3, at OKLab L 0.452, holds 5.60:1 there, which shows how much lightness margin text would need. In table view, Thunderbird applies its unread text colour only to untagged rows because tagged rows already take the tag colour ([threadPane.css](https://github.com/mozilla/releases-comm-central/blob/master/mail/themes/shared/mail/threadPane.css)), so a coloured sender would compete with tags on the same channel. Per-sender colour coding fails outright: viewers can rapidly tell apart only up to seven isoluminant colours ([Healey and Enns 2012](https://visionlab-psych.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2015/06/140_HealeyEnns_IEEE2011.pdf)), and the adjusted Nord set offers fewer than that once colour-vision deficiency is counted (next section).

A three-level row hierarchy fits inside NN/g's limit of three contrast variations ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)): the dot and sender first, the subject second, and the date, counts and icons third. The GNOME theme's 0.8 opacity on dates already lands at 6.39:1 in light mode and 9.91:1 in dark (computed), comfortably above the floor. Bolding both sender and subject on unread rows, as Gmail, Outlook and Thunderbird do, spends the emphasis on two fields; bold's effect is "inversely proportional to how often it's used" ([Fowler 2026](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/ExcessiveBold.html)), so bolding only the sender is a defensible variant, though no study shows it scans faster. Flags and tags, used only occasionally, belong in the third tier as glyphs, with any hue drawn from the warm side so they never look like the cool unread dot.

## The theme's lightness rule buys contrast and spends distinctness

Nord's official roles were written for code editors. The documentation assigns nord8 as the primary accent, nord9 and nord10 as secondary and tertiary, nord11 to errors, nord13 to warnings and nord14 to success, calls nord12 and nord15 "rarely used", and says Frost and Aurora are **"used the same for both dark & bright ambiance designs"**; it defines no link, unread or quoted-text role ([Nord docs](https://www.nordtheme.com/docs/colors-and-palettes)). Independent ports agree on error, warning, success and a nord8 link or accent, and both official editor ports brighten nord3 to #616e88 for muted text ([nord.vim](https://github.com/nordtheme/vim/blob/main/colors/nord.vim); [nord-theme.el](https://github.com/nordtheme/emacs/blob/main/nord-theme.el); [VS Code Nord](https://github.com/nordtheme/visual-studio-code/blob/develop/themes/nord-color-theme.json)). Mail-specific ports disagree: aerc uses nord7 for unread and nord14 for flagged ([aerc nord styleset](https://git.sr.ht/~rjarry/aerc/blob/master/stylesets/nord)), while mu4e uses nord13 for both ([nord-theme.el](https://github.com/nordtheme/emacs/blob/main/nord-theme.el)), so any mail mapping is an extension of the palette's documented roles.

The raw palette cannot serve light mode, and the theme's lightness rule is what fixes that. The rule, `max(l, 0.85)` in dark mode and `min(l, 0.5)` in light, holds each colour's OKLab hue and chroma while clamping its lightness ([thunderbird-gnome-theme dark.css](https://github.com/rafaelmardojai/thunderbird-gnome-theme/blob/main/theme/colors/dark.css)). Measured against the theme's backgrounds with WCAG 2.2 and the APCA reference constants ([apca-w3](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3/blob/master/src/apca-w3.js)), the trade looks like this:

| Frost and Aurora set | Light mode (four backgrounds) | Dark mode (five backgrounds) | OKLab lightness spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| Raw hex | 1.31 to 4.09:1; none reaches 4.5:1, nord7, nord8, nord13 and nord14 stay under 2.1:1 | 3.23 to 10.68:1; nord10 and nord11 never reach 4.5:1 | 0.594 to 0.855 |
| After the theme rule | 4.91 to 6.37:1; all pass 4.5:1 and APCA Lc 60 (minimum Lc 67.4) | 7.47 to 10.74:1; all pass 4.5:1 and Lc 60 (minimum Lc 65.3) | 0.499 to 0.501 light; 0.828 to 0.855 dark |

Checked against the surfaces where accents would actually appear, the adjusted set holds up everywhere except one place (computed with the [WCAG 2.2 formula](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-contrast-ratio); backgrounds from the theme's colour files and Thunderbird's dark message-pane style in [messageBody.css](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mail/themes/shared/mail/messageBody.css)):

| Surface | Light-adjusted accents | Dark-adjusted accents |
|---|---|---|
| Message list (#f3f3f5 / #28282c) | 5.28 to 5.75:1 | 8.31 to 9.46:1 |
| Selected list row (10% text tint: #e0e0e2 / #3e3e41) | **4.44 to 4.83:1** | 6.03 to 6.87:1 |
| Message body (#ffffff / #18181b) | 5.85 to 6.37:1 | 10.03 to 11.41:1 |

The price is distinctness. Clamping every colour to one lightness produces exactly the palette Okabe and Ito warn against: "Avoid the combination of colors that have the same brightness but different only in hue" ([Okabe and Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)). Simulating dichromacy with the Machado matrices at full severity ([Machado et al.](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html)) and measuring CIEDE2000 distances, where CSS Color 4 sets one just-noticeable difference at ΔE2000 2 or ΔE_OK 0.02 ([CSS Color 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#binsearch)), gives four results that drive every mapping decision. **nord9 and nord10 become one colour**, ΔE2000 3.0 even for normal vision and ΔE_OK 0.019 to 0.020, at the threshold. nord8 against nord9 drops to 2.7 to 4.4 for every deficiency type. **nord11 against nord14, error against success, falls from 15.2 raw to 5.6 (dark) and 2.4 (light) under deuteranopia**, because the rule erases the 0.162 lightness gap that let the raw pair survive, and nord12 against nord14 collapses to 1.6 and 3.0. nord15 merges into the Frost blues under protan and deutan vision (2.5 to 4.3). What survives every vision type in both modes is the cool-warm axis: the blues against nord13 stay at 31.6 to 39.0, the blues against nord12 at 24.6 to 33.1, nord14 against nord15 at 23.6 to 24.1, and nord11 against the blues at 14.6 to 23.2. Even raw, Nord is a dimmed palette whose closest pairs under protanopia and tritanopia (4.7 and 3.5) sit roughly a third of the Okabe-Ito benchmark's (12.3 and 11.1). Red-green deficiency affects about **8% of men of European descent and 0.4% of women** ([Birch 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22472762/)), so these collapses reach real readers, though severity-1.0 simulation overstates the milder anomalous forms that make up most cases.

Dark mode deserves its own margin, and the rule happens to supply it. Controlled studies find a positive-polarity advantage for both younger and older adults that **grows as characters shrink** ([Piepenbrock et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25141597/)) and is worst for light-on-dark text in a dark room ([Dobres et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/)), which makes small light accents on dark surfaces the hardest case. Worked through APCA's reference constants, light-on-dark text needs roughly 2.5 to 3 times the WCAG ratio of dark-on-light text for equal readability ([apca-w3](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)); the adjusted dark accents land at Lc 65 to 76, consistent with Material's advice to use lighter, desaturated tones on dark surfaces ([Material Design 2](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)) and Apple's target of 7:1 "especially in small text" ([Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)). APCA remains a second opinion: the W3C's 10 September 2026 WCAG 3 draft leaves the contrast algorithm "yet to be determined" and does not name APCA ([WCAG 3.0 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-wcag-3.0-20260910/)). Nord's low chroma (OKLCH 0.048 to 0.121) also avoids Material's warning that saturated colours "vibrate" on dark backgrounds. One side effect matters for muted text: the rule lifts nord3 to #c2cee6 at L 0.85, level with every accent, so a dark-mode muted tone has to come from opacity on neutral text, not from nord3.

Two readings of these numbers are open. Keeping the rule means using few hues on opposite axes and never asking two Frost colours to mean different things. Replacing the flat clamp with one that preserves a lightness offset between roles would restore the luminance channel that both colour-vision science and Okabe and Ito favour; that variant is an inference and was not measured. One technical unknown sits under both: Gecko's handling of out-of-gamut relative `oklab()` colours, clip or CSS gamut mapping, was not confirmed, which changes nord11's dark value from #ffadb4 to #ffb1b7.

## Neutral quotes, underlined links and mode-tuned signatures ease reading

The message header needs hierarchy from size and weight, with little or no hue. NN/g limits a design to three sizes and three contrast variations ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/)), and Apple advises adjusting "font weight, size, and color as needed" while avoiding light weights ([Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Thunderbird already encodes that ladder: a bold subject (weight 500 at 1.35em with the large-subject option) and field labels dimmed by a single `--message-header-label-opacity: 0.7`, with the Subject, From and Tags labels exempt ([messageHeader.css](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mail/themes/shared/mail/messageHeader.css)). In light mode that opacity has less room than it seems. If labels inherit the theme's 80%-alpha text colour, the two alphas stack to about 4.93:1 on white (computed), close to the 4.5:1 floor that applies because labels and dates are real content ([W3C, Understanding 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)), so labels should not be dimmed further. The nord-emacs mail faces show the opposite approach, giving header names nord7, the subject nord8 and To and Cc nord9 ([nord-theme.el](https://github.com/nordtheme/emacs/blob/main/nord-theme.el)); after the theme rule those three hues sit 7.0 to 14.8 apart for normal vision in dark mode and only 2.5 to 3.4 apart under deficiency, which is many colours with little separation. One accent at most belongs in the header.

Thunderbird's stock quote styling breaks two of the evidence's rules at once. Its 2px bars cycle five Tango colours by nesting depth, and on white they measure 2.77, 3.29, 1.61, 1.85 and 1.81:1, so only the second level clears 3:1. Since Bug 2018015, the quoted text itself is forced to `--color-text-highlight`, #1373d9 in light and #58c9ff in dark, with `!important` ([messageQuotes.css](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mail/themes/shared/mail/messageQuotes.css); [Bug 2018015](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2018015)). In dark mode that is the same token Thunderbird uses for links, so quoted prose and links share one blue, against NN/g's long-standing advice that blue carries "the strongest perceived affordance of clickability" ([NN/g, Visualizing links](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/guidelines-for-visualizing-links/)). Other clients use fewer steps: aerc alternates cyan and blue and dims from the third level ([aerc-stylesets](https://git.sr.ht/~rjarry/aerc/tree/master/item/doc/aerc-stylesets.7.scd)), mutt cycles user-defined colours and offers a toggle to hide quotes ([mutt pager.c](https://gitlab.com/muttmua/mutt/-/blob/master/pager.c)), and Gmail collapses repeated text behind an ellipsis ([Allegheny College](https://sites.allegheny.edu/library/?p=16642)). **No controlled usability study of quote colouring, bar width or useful depth was found.**

The vision science points to neutral quoted text and bars that vary in lightness, with hue as an extra. A 2px bar spans about 2.6 arcminutes, near the limit of chromatic resolution, where red-green and blue-yellow acuity both top out at 11 or 12 cycles per degree ([Mullen 1985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3999044/)), and Okabe and Ito note that colour-blind readers find "thin lines and small symbols" especially hard ([Okabe and Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)). A Frost-only cycle would collapse after the theme rule, but the message body is a separate document that the rule does not reach, so its bars can use real lightness steps. Two patterns fit the evidence: one Frost hue stepped down in lightness per level, aerc's model, or two hues from opposite axes, a blue alternating with nord12 or nord13. Depth is already redundant with indentation and the attribution line, so the bars arguably fall outside SC 1.4.11, but 3:1 costs nothing: adjusted accents give 5.85 to 6.37:1 on white and 10.03 to 11.41:1 on the dark body. Widening bars to 3 or 4 px helps hue survive.

The markup makes one rule cover most mail. Thunderbird turns every `>` level in plain-text and format=flowed mail into a nested `blockquote type=cite` inside `div.moz-text-plain` or `div.moz-text-flowed` ([mimetpla.cpp](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mailnews/mime/src/mimetpla.cpp); [mimetpfl.cpp](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mailnews/mime/src/mimetpfl.cpp)), and Thunderbird-sent and Fastmail HTML replies use the same element, which Apple Mail's WebKit editor also defines as its mail quote ([fastmail-cli draft.go](https://github.com/marckohlbrugge/fastmail-cli/blob/main/internal/jmap/draft.go); [WebKit Editing.cpp](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/main/Source/WebCore/editing/Editing.cpp)). Gmail wraps quotes in `blockquote.gmail_quote` with an inline 1px #ccc left border, so only its border colour should be overridden ([Gmail sample](https://github.com/grqz/grqz/blob/HEAD/email/0028.md)), and Outlook does not wrap quotes at all, leaving only its separator to restyle ([talon html_quotations.py](https://github.com/mailgun/talon/blob/master/talon/html_quotations.py)). Because `userContent.css` loads in the user origin, each declaration needs `!important` to beat Thunderbird's author sheets and senders' inline styles ([CSS Cascade 5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-cascade-5/#cascade-sort)).

Signatures show how WCAG ratios mislead under dark polarity. Thunderbird dims a plain-text signature, opened by an exact `-- ` line ([RFC 3676](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc3676#section-4.3)), to **opacity 0.6** ([messageQuotes.css](https://searchfox.org/comm-central/source/mail/themes/shared/mail/messageQuotes.css)). In light mode that gives 5.74:1 and APCA Lc 78.8. On the dark message body, #eeeef0 text on #18181b, the same opacity gives a similar 6.16:1 but only Lc 45.6, below APCA's level for any content text; opacity 0.75 restores Lc 63.0 at 8.95:1 (computed with the [APCA reference constants](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3/blob/master/src/apca-w3.js)). If APCA's polarity model is right, the stock opacity dims dark-mode signatures far more than light-mode ones, which argues for a separate value per mode. HTML signatures (`.gmail_signature`, `#Signature`, `[data-signatureblock]`) are not dimmed by Thunderbird and can be targeted with colour or size alone ([odoo mail.py](https://github.com/odoo/odoo/blob/master/odoo/tools/mail.py)); a signature whose separator lacks the trailing space escapes detection, and no CSS can repair that.

Links need an underline in dark mode, and the measurements make it mandatory. WCAG accepts colour-only links only when the link colour differs from the surrounding text by 3:1 (G183), and a cue that appears only on hover or focus fails (F73) ([W3C G183](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G183); [W3C F73](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F73)). Against #eeeef0 body text, every dark-adjusted Nord accent measures **1.34 to 1.36:1**, and Thunderbird's own #58c9ff only 1.62:1 (computed); in light mode the adjusted accents reach 3.50 to 3.58:1 against black text, a narrow pass. G183 itself recommends an underline "especially for dark colors". NN/g's March 2026 note relaxes its old underline rule for web navigation but still points to G183 for accessibility ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/guidelines-for-visualizing-links/)). A persistent underline, with a single Frost accent as the link colour, matches the port consensus of nord8 for links ([VS Code Nord](https://github.com/nordtheme/visual-studio-code/blob/develop/themes/nord-color-theme.json)). Outlook mail ships its own blue and purple underline styles in a `<style>` block, so normalising them also needs `!important` ([talon ms_outlook_2010.html](https://github.com/mailgun/talon/blob/master/tests/fixtures/html_replies/ms_outlook_2010.html)).

## A role map that survives both modes and colour-blind eyes

Read together, the findings support a small map in which each role owns a non-colour carrier and the hues come from different families. The table gives the evidence-backed choice and what the same evidence rules out; where two swatches are equally supported, the pick is taste.

| Role | Colour (through the theme rule unless noted) | Non-colour carrier | Ruled out by the evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unread (list) | nord8, on the dot only | Restored leading dot, bold sender and subject | Colour-only unread text; a 600-to-700 weight step as the sole cue |
| New (just arrived) | A warm accent (nord13) on the sparkle icon | Sparkle glyph, distinct from the dot | A second blue (nord9 or nord10) beside a nord8 dot |
| Sender | None; neutral text at full contrast | Left position, width for 20 to 25 characters, bold when unread | Per-sender hues; accent-coloured senders in table view, where tags colour the row |

## Conclusion

The most useful shift is in where colour does its work. With a pastel palette, putting colour on text always lowers luminance contrast relative to neutral text, so in this theme hue can never be the emphasis channel; it earns its place on marks (dots, bars, glyphs, underlines) whose presence or shape already carries the meaning. The theme's lightness rule sharpens that conclusion. It makes every Nord colour legible by turning the palette into an equal-brightness set, the one arrangement colour-vision guidance warns against, so the rule decides which roles can share a screen as much as it decides contrast. A design that wants more than two or three distinguishable accents has to give back some lightness spread; adding hues will not help.

Dark mode is not a mirror of light mode. The same opacity, the same WCAG ratio, or the same link colour behaves differently under negative polarity: signatures fade further, links stop separating from text, and small light marks are the hardest case in the polarity research. Each dim and each accent therefore needs a value tuned per mode and checked on the surfaces where it actually appears, including selected rows, which the light theme tints enough to push three accents under the text minimum. Because the mail-specific evidence comes from practice and measurement, with no experiment behind it, the decisive remaining test is empirical: a squint test and a deuteranopia simulation of real screenshots of the list and a nested reply in both modes.
