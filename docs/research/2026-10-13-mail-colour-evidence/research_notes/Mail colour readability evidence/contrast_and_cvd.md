# Contrast, polarity and colour-vision evidence for coloured text, small glyphs and thin bars in a desktop email client (light and dark backgrounds)

Provenance tags used below: **[STD]** = W3C/ISO-type standard or normative spec; **[PEER]** = peer-reviewed paper (abstract read via PubMed/E-utilities unless noted); **[VENDOR]** = platform design guidance; **[ADVOCACY]** = source written by the proponent of the thing it describes (APCA/Myndex); **[CALC]** = my own arithmetic using the cited published formulas/constants (the reader should treat it as derived, not as a source finding). Research date: 2026-10-13. The Nord palette is not analysed here by design.

---

## 1. WCAG 2.2 contrast: formulas, thresholds, "large text", and criticisms

### Takeaway
WCAG 2.2 measures contrast as (L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05) on relative luminance, with 4.5:1 for normal text (SC 1.4.3 AA), 3:1 for large text, 7:1 / 4.5:1 for AAA (SC 1.4.6), and 3:1 against adjacent colours for UI components and meaning-bearing graphical objects (SC 1.4.11). The formula is hue-blind and polarity-blind by design; critics (mostly APCA's author) say it overstates contrast near black, which matters for dark mode.

### Cited Findings
- **[STD] Relative luminance:** `L = 0.2126 * R + 0.7152 * G + 0.0722 * B`, where for each channel `RsRGB = R8bit/255` and `if RsRGB <= 0.04045 then R = RsRGB/12.92 else R = ((RsRGB+0.055)/1.055) ^ 2.4`. Note 2 in the glossary: "Before May 2021 the value of 0.04045 in the definition was different (0.03928)", with no practical effect. — [WCAG 2.2 glossary, relative luminance](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-relative-luminance)
- **[STD] Contrast ratio:** `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)`, L1 the lighter colour's relative luminance, L2 the darker's; range 1 to 21 ("1:1 to 21:1"). The glossary does not explain the 0.05 term. — [WCAG 2.2 glossary, contrast ratio](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#dfn-contrast-ratio)
- **[STD] SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), AA:** text and images of text need at least 4.5:1; large-scale text at least 3:1. Large-scale = "at least 18 point or 14 point bold", which the Understanding page equates to roughly 24 px and 18.5 px (1 pt = 1.333 px). Computed values must not be rounded: 4.499:1 fails 4.5:1. Exempt: incidental text (inactive UI component, pure decoration, invisible), logotypes. — [Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[STD] Same page, hue and light-on-dark notes:** contrast is calculated so colour (hue) is not a key factor; the page cautions against predominantly long-wavelength colours (e.g. red) on darker backgrounds because of protanopia; and says thin or unusual fonts may anti-alias fainter than their defined colour, so authors should allow margin above the minimum. — [Understanding SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
- **[STD] SC 1.4.6 Contrast (Enhanced), AAA:** 7:1 for text, 4.5:1 for large-scale text; same exceptions. The page explains the derivation: 3:1 is the minimum recommended by ISO 9241-3 and ANSI/HFES 100-1988; "visual acuity of 20/40 is associated with a contrast sensitivity loss of roughly 1.5", so 3:1 x 1.5 gives about 4.5:1 (AA); 20/80 acuity gives about 7:1 (AAA). It also says colour deficiencies "can affect luminance contrast somewhat" and that luminance contrast "is independent of color perception", with the protanopia long-wavelength-on-dark exception. — [Understanding SC 1.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)
- **[STD] SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast, AA:** 3:1 "against adjacent color(s)" for (a) visual information required to identify UI components and states (inactive components exempt; includes state such as selected or focused) and (b) "parts of graphics required to understand the content" (stand-alone icons such as a print icon with no text; status icons on a dashboard without associated text need 3:1). No rounding (2.999:1 fails). The page sets no minimum size for icons or elements. It warns that "particularly thin lines and shapes of non-text elements may be rendered by user agents with a much fainter color than the actual color defined in the underlying CSS" and advises avoiding thin lines or using colours that exceed the minimum. Text-character symbols (e.g. a close "X") count as non-text and need 3:1. — [Understanding SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- **[STD] SC 1.4.1 Use of Color, level A:** colour must not be the only visual means of conveying information, indicating an action, prompting a response, or distinguishing a visual element. Technique G183 (3:1 contrast between inline link text and surrounding text) is listed as a sufficient technique for colour-distinguished links; failure F73 covers links "not visually evident without color vision". — [Understanding SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
- **[ADVOCACY] Criticism for dark colours:** APCA's author states WCAG 2.x "overstates contrast for dark colors to the point that 4.5:1 can be functionally unreadable when one of the colors in a pair is near black. As a result, WCAG 2.x contrast cannot be used for guidance designing 'dark mode'." — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html); same claim in [Why APCA](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/WhyAPCA.html)
- **[ADVOCACY] Worked example in a W3C list thread:** the pair under discussion is #a0a0a0 and #000 (WCAG ratio 8:1 in either polarity). Somers calls WCAG's 8:1 for it "a misnomer, WCAG 2.x is simply wrong", but his own example is black text on a #a0a0a0 background (his linked APCA test uses BG=a0a0a0, TXT=000000): he says that if WCAG 2.x were perceptually accurate it "would be less than 4.5:1", and that black text on #909090 "would be an equivalent of about 3:1". The list participant he replies to (Jonathan Avila, Level Access) accepts 8:1 as what the current algorithm yields but says "8:1 contrast isn't enough for some users with low vision". — [W3C low-vision TF list, 2021-09-21](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-low-vision-a11y-tf/2021Sep/0011.html)
- **[ADVOCACY, unverified] "47% that pass WCAG 2 should fail" claim:** repeated in W3C list posts by the APCA author, but I could not locate the cited independent review. — [W3C silver list 2023-08](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-silver/2023Aug/0018.html) (post exists; the statistic is unverified)

### Inferences
- **[CALC]** Using the WCAG formula above I reproduced the Somers/Avila pair: #a0a0a0 against #000 = 8.03:1 in either polarity (APCA reference code gives Lc 53.0 for black text on #a0a0a0 and Lc -50.9 for #a0a0a0 text on black, and Lc 44.9 for black text on #909090 where WCAG says 6.58:1, so APCA does put the pair far below WCAG's 8:1, in line with Somers's claim, though that is APCA agreeing with itself, not independent evidence). The same formula gives mid-greys that pass 4.5:1 on both polarities: #767676 on white = 4.54:1, #757575 on black = 4.56:1. The WCAG ratio is therefore symmetric between a light and a dark background for the same grey, which is exactly the property critics dispute (see section 2 for APCA's view of the same pairs).
- The 0.05 term acts like a flare/ambient-light offset, but no primary W3C rationale was found (see Gaps), so do not cite it as measured.
- For coloured accents the practical consequence is: to pass SC 1.4.3 on white an accent needs relative luminance Y of about 0.18 or lower (4.5:1) or 0.30 or lower (3:1); on a near-black (#121212, Y = 0.006) it needs Y of about 0.20 or higher (4.5:1) or 0.12 or higher (3:1). **[CALC]** from the formula.

### Gaps
- No primary W3C explanation of the 0.05 constant found. A secondary blog (Neil Bickford, neilbickford.com/blog/?p=1051, seen only as a search snippet) says it models screen black-level plus reflected ambient light; unverified.
- No independent, peer-reviewed validation study of WCAG 2 contrast ratios against readability data was found in this session; the criticism comes from the APCA author. A 2025 Croatian conference paper (39 participants) reportedly favours APCA but is small and I saw only the listing: [TVZ listing](https://www2.tvz.hr/publikacija/accessibility-in-user-interface-design-the-role-of-contrast/).

## 2. APCA (Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm): Lc values, polarity, 2026 status

### Takeaway
APCA is a polarity-aware lightness-contrast model (Lc, about 0 to 105) with use-case thresholds: Lc 90 preferred / 75 minimum for body text, 60 for other content text, 45 for large text and fine icons, 30 for spot text and solid semantic icons, 15 for non-semantic non-text. As of the W3C WCAG 3 Working Draft of 10 September 2026 it is not named in the specification and the contrast algorithm is explicitly "yet to be determined".

### Cited Findings
- **[ADVOCACY] What it is:** "Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm is a new method for calculating and predicting readability contrast", part of the S-Luv/SACAM model, "optimized for accessible color appearance on self-illuminated RGB computer displays". It reports Lc "from Lc 0 to Lc 105+"; "consider Lc 15 the point of invisibility for many users, and Lc 90 as preferred for body text". — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)
- **[ADVOCACY] Use-case thresholds (Bronze Simple Mode), verbatim levels:**
  - Lc 90: preferred for fluent text and body columns, font no smaller than 18px/weight 300 or 14px/400 (non-body text 12px/400); suggested maximum for very large bold fonts.
  - Lc 75: "minimum level for columns of body text" at 24px/300, 18px/400, 16px/500, 14px/700; usable for non-body text at 15px/400.
  - Lc 60: "minimum level recommended for content text that is not body"; sizes 48px/200, 36px/300, 24px/400, 21px/500, 18px/600, 16px/700; "To use these sizes as body text, add Lc 15."
  - Lc 45: minimum for larger heavier text (36px/400 or 24px bold) "This is also the minimum for pictograms with fine details, or smaller outline icons."
  - Lc 30: "absolute minimum for any text not listed above" (spot-readable, placeholder, disabled) and "the minimum for large/solid semantic & understandable non-text elements such as 'mostly solid' icons or pictograms. Generally no less than 5.5px solid in its smallest dimension."
  - Lc 15: "absolute minimum for any non-semantic non-text that needs to be discernible, and is no less than 5px (solid) in its smallest dimension"; "Designers should treat anything below this level as invisible". "For the equivelent to AAA, simply increase the contrast values by Lc 15."
  - Font-size assumptions: x-height ratio 0.52, Helvetica/Arial reference weights; "One reference px is defined as 1.278 arc minutes of visual angle." — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)
- **[ADVOCACY] Lookup-table notes:** for body text add Lc 15 to any table value below Lc 75; sub-fluent text may be lowered by Lc 15 but never below Lc 30; non-fluent spot text by Lc 25 but never below Lc 30; fonts over 24px at weight 300+ have a maximum of Lc 90. — [apca-w3 README, font lookup table](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)
- **Polarity in the algorithm (reference code, constants "0.0.98G-4g", 15 Feb 2021):** exponents `normBG 0.56, normTXT 0.57` (dark text on light) versus `revTXT 0.62, revBG 0.65` (light text on dark); `scaleBoW = scaleWoB = 1.14`; low-contrast offset 0.027; near-black soft clamp `blkThrs 0.022, blkClmp 1.414`; luminance from sRGB with a plain 2.4 exponent and coefficients 0.2126729 / 0.7151522 / 0.0721750. Light-on-dark intentionally returns a negative Lc ("Do not swap, polarity is important"). Inputs are text colour first, background second. — [apca-w3 source](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3/blob/master/src/apca-w3.js), [README](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)
- **[ADVOCACY] Perceptual-uniformity claim:** "regardless of how light or dark the colors are, a contrast value of Lc 60 represents the same perceived readability contrast"; APCA "pivots near the point where the CS curve flattens due to contrast constancy"; it includes "a small boost for very dark colors". — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)
- **[STD, status] WCAG 3 draft:** W3C Working Draft dated 10 September 2026. Requirement "Text contrast sufficient (minimum)" is "meets @@[contrast measure to be determined]". Editor's note: "The contrast algorithm used in WCAG 3 is yet to be determined. For this draft, the requirement assumes the algorithm will include a size/weight factor ... A separate requirement may be needed if red/green color vision deficiency (CVD) is not accounted for within the contrast algorithm." The draft's "contrast ratio test" glossary entry is "Exploratory", defined by "the relationship of hue, saturation, and lightness values". A text search of the full draft for "APCA" returned zero hits. "Graphical object contrast sufficient" is the non-text requirement (Developing). Conformance tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold) are described as an exploration the AG WG is still discussing. — [WCAG 3.0 WD 2026-09-10](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-wcag-3.0-20260910/) (latest: [w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/))
- **[ADVOCACY] APCA self-description of status:** "the candidate contrast method for the future WCAG 3" and also developing as the independent APCA Readability Criterion hosted by Inclusive Reading Technologies; the apca-w3 README disclaimer says standards that will incorporate APCA "are still developmental". — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html), [apca-w3 README](https://github.com/Myndex/apca-w3)
- **[VENDOR] Apple recognises both:** the HIG Accessibility page names WCAG and "the Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm (APCA)" as two popular measures, but its own table uses WCAG AA values (below). — [Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- **[CALC] APCA treats WCAG-equal pairs very differently by polarity.** Using the constants above on neutral greys: on white, WCAG 3:1 / 4.5:1 / 7:1 correspond to #949494 / #767676 / #595959 with Lc 57.1 / 71.6 / 84.3. On #121212 the same WCAG ratios correspond to #616161 / #7d7d7d / #9f9f9f with Lc -20.4 / -32.8 / -49.8; on pure black, Lc -18.1 / -29.6 / -45.1. So a 4.5:1 grey on a dark background sits near APCA's Lc 30 "spot text" floor, not near its Lc 60 to 75 text levels.
- **[CALC] Equal |Lc| needs very different WCAG ratios.** Grey text reaching |Lc| 60: on white needs only 3.28:1 (#8e8e8e); on #121212 needs 8.84:1 (#b2b2b2); on #1e1e1e 8.13:1 (#b5b5b5); on black 9.79:1 (#b1b1b1). |Lc| 75: 5.10:1 on white (#6e6e6e) versus 11.67:1 on #121212 (#cccccc). If APCA's model is right, light-on-dark text needs roughly 2.5 to 3 times the WCAG ratio of dark-on-light text for equal readability.
- **[CALC] Accent sweep (OKLCH, chroma 0.12, hues 0 to 330 step 30, in-gamut only):** L = 0.55 on white gives WCAG 4.57 to 5.20 and Lc 71.5 to 75.4; L = 0.50 on white gives 5.64 to 6.41 and Lc 77.9 to 81.5; L = 0.72 on #1e1e1e gives 6.32 to 7.11 and Lc -54.4 to -48.9; L = 0.78 on #1e1e1e gives 7.89 to 8.75 and Lc -64.7 to -59.3. An accent meant to clear Lc 60 as text on a dark surface lands near OKLCH L 0.78 (WCAG about 8:1), well above what WCAG 4.5:1 would suggest.
- Because the WCAG 3 draft does not adopt APCA and the algorithm is undecided, APCA is best treated as a second opinion that is useful specifically for dark-mode polarity, not as a compliance target.
- APCA's own minimum-size notes (5 px solid for Lc 15 non-text, 5.5 px solid for Lc 30 semantic icons) are the only quantitative minimum-size guidance for non-text elements I found in a contrast framework (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No independent peer-reviewed validation of APCA found; the "APCA Reviewed" list on the project's own site was not checked and its items were not verified.
- One search summary said a 2024 AG WG co-chair statement described APCA as not in the current draft; I could not retrieve that statement. The 2026-09-10 draft text itself (above) is the reliable evidence.
- The APCA lookup-table images (font size by weight by Lc) were not machine-readable in this session; only the Bronze Simple Mode level descriptions above were captured.

## 3. Dark mode and polarity research; age, pupil size, halation; vendor guidance on saturation

### Takeaway
Controlled studies using proofreading, acuity and lexical-decision tasks consistently find a positive-polarity (dark on light) advantage for both younger and older adults, larger at small sizes, and explained best by display luminance acting through pupil size and optical aberrations; it does not vanish in the dark. The "halation for people with astigmatism" claim is a plausible mechanism but I found no controlled study isolating it. Material and Apple both advise desaturated/lighter accent tones on dark surfaces and Apple asks for 4.5:1 minimum and 7:1 where practical, especially for small text.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] Piepenbrock, Mayr, Mund & Buchner (2013), Ergonomics 56(7):1116-24, "Positive display polarity is advantageous for both younger and older adults":** visual-acuity test (black optotypes on white vs white on black) and a proofreading task. "A positive polarity advantage was found for both age groups. The presentation in positive polarity is recommended for all ages." Rationale tested: lens/vitreous light scatter in older eyes could reverse the advantage, but "age-related changes lead to a decline in retinal illuminance. Brighter positive polarity displays should help to compensate". — [PubMed 23654206](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23654206/)
- **[PEER] Piepenbrock, Mayr & Buchner (2014), Ergonomics 57(11):1670-7, "Smaller pupil size and better proofreading performance with positive than with negative polarity displays":** "pupil sizes were smaller and proofreading performance was better with positive than with negative polarity displays"; compatible with the display luminance hypothesis (greater brightness gives smaller pupil, "a sharper retinal image and better perception of detail"). — [PubMed 25135324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25135324/)
- **[PEER] Piepenbrock, Mayr & Buchner (2014), Human Factors 56(5):942-51, "Positive display polarity is particularly advantageous for small character sizes":** black-on-white vs white-on-black proofreading at 8, 10, 12 and 14 pt (0.22, 0.25, 0.31, 0.34 degrees of vertical visual angle). "the positive polarity advantage linearly increased with decreasing character size ... Especially with small font sizes, negative polarity displays should be avoided." — [PubMed 25141597](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25141597/)
- **[PEER] Buchner & Baumgartner (2007), Ergonomics 50(7):1036-63:** proofreading better with positive polarity, "independent of ambient lighting (darkness vs. typical office illumination) and of chromaticity (black and white vs. blue and yellow)"; a final experiment "showed that colour contrast (red text on green background) could not compensate for a lack of luminance contrast"; physiological effort measures and self-reported fatigue/eyestrain did not vary with condition. — [PubMed 17510822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17510822/)
- **[PEER] Dobres, Chahine, Reimer (2017), Applied Ergonomics 60:68-73:** glance-like lexical decision, two sizes, two polarities, near-dark vs daylight-like ambient light. Legibility thresholds were worst for negative polarity under dark ambient illumination; positive polarity in the dark and all conditions under bright illumination were significantly better. Interpreted as consistent with brighter illumination producing "pupillary contraction that reduces optical aberrations as light enters the eye". — [PubMed 28166901](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/)
- **[PEER] Dobres et al. (2016), Ergonomics 59(10):1377-91:** "black-on-white text is significantly more legible than white-on-black" under glance-like reading; typeface and age interacted. — [PubMed 26727912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26727912/)
- **[PEER, counter-datum] Somkijrungroj et al. (2026), Clin Ophthalmol 20:617151:** 26 patients with bilateral diffractive multifocal IOLs; non-randomised; reading performance "did not differ significantly between positive and negative display polarities at 60 cm (p = 0.055)". Population is pseudophakic, not general. — [PubMed 42741702](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42741702/)
- **[VENDOR] Material Design 2 dark theme (page text rendered and read directly):**
  - "Contrast: Dark surfaces and 100% white body text have a contrast level of at least 15.8:1"; surfaces "should use a contrast level of at least 15.8:1 between text and the background. This ensures that body text passes WCAG's AA standard of at least 4.5:1 when applied to surfaces at the highest (and lightest) elevation." Recommended dark surface #121212.
  - "Desaturation: Primary colors are desaturated so they pass the ... WCAG AA standard of at least 4.5:1 (when used with body text) at all elevation levels."
  - "A dark theme should avoid using saturated colors, as they don't pass WCAG's accessibility standard of at least 4.5:1 for body text against dark surfaces. Saturated colors also produce optical vibrations against a dark background, which can induce eye strain. Instead, desaturated colors can be used as a more legible alternative."
  - "it's recommended to use lighter tones (200-50) in dark theme, rather than your default color theme (saturated tones ranging from 900-500)"; baseline dark primary is the 200 tone.
  - Light text on dark: high-emphasis text opacity 87%, medium-emphasis and hint 60%, disabled 38%.
  — [Material Design 2: Dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)
- **[VENDOR] Material Components Android:** "The baseline Material Dark theme background and surface colors are dark grey instead of black, which increases visibility for shadows and also reduces eye strain for light text." — [Dark.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Dark.md)
- **[VENDOR] Material HCT tone rule (source-code comment):** "A difference of 40 in HCT tone guarantees a contrast ratio >= 3.0, and a difference of 50 guarantees a contrast ratio >= 4.5." Tone is CIE L*; their contrast function is (lighter+5)/(darker+5) on Y 0 to 100, i.e. the WCAG formula. — [material-color-utilities hct.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/hct/hct.ts), [contrast.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/contrast/contrast.ts)
- **[VENDOR] Apple HIG, Dark Mode:** "Aim for sufficient color contrast in all appearances ... At a minimum, make sure the contrast ratio between colors is no lower than 4.5:1. For custom foreground and background colors, strive for a contrast ratio of 7:1, especially in small text." Dark Mode palette "includes dimmer background colors and brighter foreground colors ... these colors aren't necessarily inversions of their light counterparts". For custom colours, "add a Color Set asset ... and specify the bright and dim variants", avoid hard-coded colours. "Soften the color of white backgrounds" so they do not glow. Warns that Increase Contrast in Dark Mode "can result in reduced visual contrast between dark text and a dark background". — [Apple HIG: Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)
- **[VENDOR] Apple HIG, Color:** supply "light and dark variants, and an increased contrast option for each variant"; "In bright surroundings, colors look darker and more muted. In dark environments, colors appear bright and saturated." — [Apple HIG: Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)
- **[VENDOR] Apple HIG, Accessibility minimum-contrast table (from WCAG AA):** text up to 17 pt, all weights: 4.5:1; 18 pt, all weights: 3:1; bold, all sizes: 3:1. Also: pairings such as "red-green and blue-orange" are hard for people who are colour blind; "Offer visual indicators, like distinct shapes or icons, in addition to color". — [Apple HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

### Inferences
- The peer-reviewed polarity literature tests black-on-white vs white-on-black body text, mostly proofreading and acuity; none of it tests coloured accents, small glyphs or thin bars. Extending it to accent colours is an inference: the mechanism (luminance, pupil size, aberrations, size dependence) predicts small light-on-dark items (dots, 11 to 12 px sender names) are the worst case, and Piepenbrock 2014's size interaction says so explicitly for text.
- Buchner & Baumgartner's red-on-green result and Dobres's ambient-light result support giving dark-mode accents extra luminance margin rather than relying on hue to carry legibility.
- Material's "saturated colours vibrate" and Apple's "bright and dim variants" converge on the same rule: author two variants per accent, not one shared hue at one lightness. **[CALC]** from section 2: for WCAG-equal contrast the dark-mode variant needs a markedly higher lightness (OKLCH L about 0.72 to 0.78 versus about 0.50 to 0.55 on white at C = 0.12).
- Material's dark surfaces at #121212 versus pure black are vendor judgement (shadows, "eye strain"), not backed by a cited controlled study in these pages.

### Gaps
- **Halation / astigmatism:** no controlled study isolating astigmatism, pupil dilation and light-on-dark reading was found. PubMed searches combining astigmatism with dark mode/negative polarity returned nothing relevant; web results were consumer-health sites and forum posts (e.g. an attribution to a UBC group that I could not trace to a paper). The mechanism the peer-reviewed work supports is general (pupil size and aberrations), not astigmatism-specific. A 2025 paper on simulated astigmatism and reading ([Vision 9(4):99](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41441557/)) was found but its abstract does not address display polarity.
- The Bauer & Cavonius (1980) result "26% more accurate with dark characters on light" appears in secondary sites only; the original chapter was not accessed, so it is not cited as a finding.
- A 2024 PMC review of dark mode and visual fatigue (PMC11175232) was returned by search but blocked by a reCAPTCHA; unread.
- Age-by-polarity effects beyond Piepenbrock 2013 (younger vs older adults only) were not found for very old or low-vision readers.

## 4. Colour-vision deficiency: prevalence, confusions, simulation methods, practical rules

### Takeaway
About 8% of European-descent men and about 0.4 to 0.5% of women have red-green deficiency; deutan types dominate, protan about a quarter of red-green cases, tritan very rare (inherited) but blue-yellow loss also occurs with age. Hue pairs on the red-green-brown-orange axis and blue-purple / blue-green confuse; the robust channel is luminance difference, plus redundant (shape, position, text) coding. Brettel/Viénot/Mollon (dichromats) and Machado/Oliveira/Fernandes (anomalous trichromacy and dichromacy, severity-parameterised) are the standard simulation models.

### Cited Findings
- **[PEER] Prevalence of red-green deficiency (Birch 2012, JOSA A 29(3):313-20):** "prevalence of deficiency in European Caucasians is about 8% in men and about 0.4% in women and between 4% and 6.5% in men of Chinese and Japanese ethnicity"; male:female ratio differs markedly between Europeans and Asians; prevalence reported rising in men of African ethnicity and in migrant-settled areas; attributed to founder events and genetic drift. — [PubMed 22472762](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22472762/)
- **[ADVOCACY/charity, approximate] By type among colour-blind men (Colour Blind Awareness):** the 8% of men with inherited colour blindness "can be divided approximately into 1% deuteranopes, 1% protanopes, 1% protanomalous and 5% deuteranomalous"; "worldwide 8% of men and 0.5% of women have a red/green type"; Scandinavia about 10 to 11% of men; "Numbers of tritanopes/tritanomalous people and achromats is very small, perhaps 1 in 30-50,000"; achromatopsia about 1 in 33,000; acquired deficiency "as many as 3% of the population", common over age 65. Roughly half of those affected have a mild anomalous form. — [Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/types-of-colour-blindness/)
- **[VENDOR/gov] NEI types:** protanomaly "makes certain shades of red look more green and less bright"; deuteranomaly makes some greens look more red; tritanomaly makes it hard to tell blue from green and yellow from red; tritanopia: blue/green, purple/red, yellow/pink; the page gives no prevalence figures. — [NEI](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness/types-color-vision-deficiency)
- **Confusion pairs (Colour Blind Awareness):** protanopes confuse black with many reds, dark brown with dark green/orange/red/blue-purple, some blues with some reds/purples/dark pinks, mid-greens with some oranges; deuteranopes confuse mid-reds with mid-greens, blue-greens with grey and mid-pinks, bright greens with yellows, pale pinks with light grey/white, mid-reds with mid-brown, light blues with lilac; tritanopes confuse light blues with greys, dark purples with black, mid-greens with blues, oranges with reds; protan/deutan anomalous types "commonly confuse blue and purple hues". — [Colour Blind Awareness](https://www.colourblindawareness.org/colour-blindness/types-of-colour-blindness/)
- **Protan brightness loss:** Okabe & Ito: "Especially for protanopes, who cannot detect long wavelength of red light, dark red appears almost as black ... black text and red text look the same for protanopes. ... avoid using red characters on black backgrounds". WCAG 1.4.3/1.4.6 flag the same long-wavelength-on-dark exception. — [Okabe & Ito, Color Universal Design](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/); [Understanding 1.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)
- **[PEER] Brettel, Viénot & Mollon (1997), JOSA A 14(10):2647-55, "Computerized simulation of color appearance for dichromats":** algorithm in LMS space projecting each stimulus onto a "reduced stimulus surface" defined by a neutral axis and anchor wavelengths: yellow 575 nm and blue 475 nm for protan and deutan, red 660 nm and blue-green 485 nm for tritan; validated by a protanope and a deuteranope accepting the matches. — [PubMed 9316278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9316278/)
- **[PEER] Viénot, Brettel & Mollon (1999), Color Research and Application 24(4):243-252 (DOI 10.1002/(SICI)1520-6378(199908)24:4<243::AID-COL5>3.0.CO;2-3):** "Digital video colourmaps for checking the legibility of displays by dichromats"; the R `dichromat` package cites its Table 2 for the protan/deutan mapping and Brettel 1997 for tritan. Citation confirmed only via package documentation, abstract not read. — [dichromat reference manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/dichromat/refman/dichromat.html)
- **[PEER] Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009), IEEE TVCG 15(6):1291-8:** physiologically based model "the first model to consistently handle normal color vision, anomalous trichromacy, and dichromacy in a unified way", validated with CVD and normal observers; cites CVD as affecting "approximately 200 million people worldwide". — [PubMed 19834201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19834201/)
- **Machado severity-1.0 matrices (RGB, severity 1.0 = dichromacy; table available 0.0 to 1.0 in steps of 0.1, interpolate for intermediate severity):** protanopia `[0.152286 1.052583 -0.204868; 0.114503 0.786281 0.099216; -0.003882 -0.048116 1.051998]`; deuteranopia `[0.367322 0.860646 -0.227968; 0.280085 0.672501 0.047413; -0.011820 0.042940 0.968881]`; tritanopia `[1.255528 -0.076749 -0.178779; -0.078411 0.930809 0.147602; 0.004733 0.691367 0.303900]`. The authors' page does not state the colour space (see Gaps). — [Machado et al. supplementary page](https://www.inf.ufrgs.br/~oliveira/pubs_files/CVD_Simulation/CVD_Simulation.html)
- **Practical rules, Okabe & Ito (Color Universal Design, 2002, modified 2008):** "Avoid the combination of colors that have the same brightness but different only in hue. For example, red characters on green backgrounds is unreadable for colorblinds. Use either bright texts/objects over dark backgrounds, or vice versa." "Make texts and objects as thick or big as possible. When the size of color-coded objects is small, only a few cone cells can be used for recognition. Colorblind people find it especially difficult to distinguish the colors of thin lines and small symbols. For color-coded text, use bold fonts such as Arial or Helvetica, rather than thin Times or NewYork." "Use not only different colors but also a combination of different shapes, positions, line types and coloring patterns." "do not use the combination of red and green. Use magenta (purple) and green instead." Authors are both protanopes. — [Okabe & Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)
- **[PEER] Buchner & Baumgartner (2007):** red text on green could not compensate for lacking luminance contrast (normal-vision observers). — [PubMed 17510822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17510822/)
- **[STD] WCAG 1.4.1 and Apple HIG:** colour alone must not carry information; give a shape, glyph or text label (Apple names "red-green and blue-orange" as risky pairings). — [Understanding 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html); [Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- **WCAG 3 draft acknowledges the gap:** a separate requirement "may be needed if red/green color vision deficiency (CVD) is not accounted for within the contrast algorithm". — [WCAG 3.0 WD 2026-09-10](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-wcag-3.0-20260910/)

### Inferences
- Luminance contrast is robust because L-M opponent (red-green) and S (blue-yellow) hue signals are the channels that fail in CVD while the luminance channel is preserved (consistent with Okabe & Ito, WCAG 1.4.6 "independent of color perception", and Buchner & Baumgartner). The one hue-dependent exception is protan dimming of long-wavelength red, which means "red" accents need a lighter, less pure (more orange/pink) variant on dark backgrounds.
- Two accents that differ mainly in hue (red vs green; blue vs purple; green vs brown/orange) should also differ in OKLCH lightness by a clear margin and carry a non-colour cue (glyph shape, position, label). A hue-only distinction among unread dot, flagged icon and quote-level bar colours is the scenario the sources warn against.
- Because roughly 8% of men are affected and about 5% of men are deuteranomalous, protan and deutan simulation (Machado severity 1.0 plus intermediate severities such as 0.6) should be the minimum check; tritan is rare inherited but blue-yellow loss increases with age (acquired CVD "common in the over 65s"), so keep blue-versus-yellow/green-versus-blue distinctions luminance-backed as well.

### Gaps
- Birch 2012 reports only the red-green total. Per-type percentages (1/1/1/5) come from a charity site, not a primary paper; the primary population-genetics source (e.g. Sharpe/Nathans/Deeb reviews) was not retrieved. Inherited tritan prevalence has only the rough "1 in 30-50,000" combined with achromats.
- The Viénot 1999 abstract and Table 2 were not read.
- The Machado page does not state whether its matrices apply to linear-light or gamma-encoded RGB; the paper itself was not fetched. Implementations commonly apply them to linearised sRGB, but I did not verify that in a primary source.
- The Okabe-Ito palette's hex values were not retrievable from a fetched primary source (the Jfly page shows it only as an image; the Nature Methods "Points of view" article is behind a login redirect).

## 5. Perceptually uniform colour spaces (OKLab/OKLCH, CIELAB L*) versus HSL; CSS support including Gecko / Thunderbird 158

### Takeaway
HSL lightness is not perceptual (the W3C CSS Color spec itself says blue and yellow at hsl(... 100% 50%) have the same HSL lightness but yellow "clearly ... looks much lighter"), so holding a hue while shifting lightness for light versus dark backgrounds is far better done in OKLCH (or CIELAB LCH). oklab()/oklch() ship in Firefox 113, relative colour syntax in Firefox 128 (with `from currentColor` in 133), and the Thunderbird build on this machine is Gecko 158, so all of it is available in Thunderbird 158.

### Cited Findings
- **[STD] CSS Color 4 on HSL vs OkLCh:** "A disadvantage of HSL over OkLCh is that hue manipulation changes the visual lightness, and that hues are not evenly spaced apart ... because the lightness is simply the mean of the gamma-corrected red, green and blue components it does not correspond to the visual perception of lightness across hues. For example, blue is represented in HSL as hsl(240deg 100% 50%) while yellow is hsl(60deg 100% 50%). Both have an HSL Lightness of 50%, but clearly the yellow looks much lighter than the blue. In OkLCh, sRGB blue is oklch(0.452 0.313 264.1) while sRGB yellow is oklch(0.968 0.211 109.8)." Also: "The hue angle in HSL is not perceptually uniform", with a worked pair. "Because Oklab is more perceptually uniform than CIE Lab, the color difference is a straightforward distance in 3D space". Status: W3C Candidate Recommendation Draft, 9 October 2026. — [CSS Color Module Level 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/)
- **[STD] CSS Color 4 on CIELAB:** "L=50% or 50 is mid gray, by design, and equal increments in L are evenly spaced visually: the Lab color space is intended to be perceptually uniform"; equal steps in luminance are "equally spaced in light energy but not equally spaced visually". — [CSS Color Module Level 4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/)
- **[STD, draft] CSS Color 5:** adds `color-mix()`, `light-dark()`, `contrast-color()` and relative colour syntax (sections 4.1 to 4.10 include Relative Oklab and Relative OkLCh Colors). Status: W3C Working Draft, 13 September 2026. — [CSS Color Module Level 5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-5/)
- **Oklab (Ottosson, published 23 Dec 2020):** goal "a perceptual color space ... simple to use, while doing a good job at predicting perceived lightness, chroma and hue"; use cases include "Increasing the saturation of colors, while maintaining perceived hue and lightness". On CIELAB/CIELUV: "Largest issue is their inability to predict hue. In particular blue hues are predicted badly." "CIELAB, CIELUV and HSV all show hue shifts towards purple" in blends. Reference data: his comparison table is generated from CAM16-UCS predictions, not human experiments, and he states it "can't be used to say which model best matches human perception"; against that reference, L RMS: Oklab 0.20, CIELAB 1.70, HSV 11.59; H RMS: Oklab 0.49, CIELAB 0.69, IPT 0.48, JzAzBz 0.43, HSV 1.10. In a HSV gradient "Yellow, magenta and cyan appear much lighter than red and blue." Oklab is "Part of CSS Color Level 4 and 5, supported by major browsers" (2025 introduction). — [Ottosson, A perceptual color space for image processing](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)
- **[VENDOR] Material HCT:** uses CIE L* as "tone" so that tone differences map to guaranteed contrast ratios (40 gives at least 3.0, 50 gives at least 4.5). — [hct.ts](https://github.com/material-foundation/material-color-utilities/blob/main/typescript/hct/hct.ts)
- **Browser support (MDN browser-compat-data v8.1.5, snapshot 2026-10-08):** `oklch()` and `oklab()`: Firefox 113, Chrome 111, Safari 15.4. `color-mix()`: Firefox 113, Chrome 111, Safari 16.2. Relative colour syntax for `oklch()`/`oklab()`/`hsl()`/`rgb()`/`lab()`/`lch()`/`hwb()`/`color()`: Firefox 128 (Chrome 119 with later fixes to channel scaling, Safari 16.4 partial then 18). `from currentColor`: Firefox 133, Chrome 131, Safari 18. `light-dark()`: Firefox 120, Chrome 123, Safari 17.5. `contrast-color()`: Firefox 146, Chrome 147, Safari 26. — [mdn/browser-compat-data via jsDelivr](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@mdn/browser-compat-data/data.json) (project: [github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data))
- **caniuse "CSS Relative color syntax":** Firefox 133 shown as full support, 128 to 132 partial; Chrome 131 full, 119 to 130 partial; Safari 18.0 full, 16.4 to 17.6 partial; global usage 93.45% (88.09% + 5.36%). The "partial" band for Firefox 128 to 132 corresponds in BCD to missing `from currentColor`, not to broken `oklch(from ...)`. — [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/css-relative-colors)
- **MDN syntax:** `oklch(from <origin> l c h)`; in `oklch()` the channel names are `l`, `c`, `h`, `alpha`; origin may be a custom property, `currentColor` or a system colour; channels resolve to numbers, so `calc(l + 20%)` is invalid and `oklch(from var(--c) calc(l - 0.1) c h)` is the pattern (OKLCH L is a 0 to 1 number). — [MDN: Relative colors](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/CSS_colors/Relative_colors)
- **Thunderbird 158 is Gecko 158-era:** the Thunderbird Flatpak installed on this workstation reports `org.mozilla.thunderbird 158.0 stable` (local `flatpak list`, 2026-10-13). Secondary reporting says Thunderbird 153 "Meadow" (July 2026) shares the Gecko engine with Firefox 153 and that Thunderbird 157.0 and 153.4.0 ESR shipped around 2 October 2026 — [Deskmodder](https://www.deskmodder.de/blog/2026/10/02/thunderbird-157-0-153-4-0-esr-und-140-17-0-esr-mit-sicherheitskorrekturen-und-mehr/) (secondary).
- The workstation's existing Thunderbird CSS already uses `color-mix(in srgb, ...)` (grep of `thunderbird/customContent.css`), which shipped in Firefox 113.

### Inferences
- **[CALC] Neutral-grey lightness anchors (OKLab L = cube root of Y for achromatic colours; CIELAB L* from Y):** WCAG 4.5:1 on white = #767676 = OKLab L 0.566 / L* 49.6; 3:1 = #949494 = L 0.667 / L* 61.3; 7:1 = #595959 = L 0.464 / L* 37.8. On #121212 (L 0.182): 3:1 = #616161 = L 0.493; 4.5:1 = #7d7d7d = L 0.590; 7:1 = #9f9f9f = L 0.703. The Material 40/50 rule of thumb checks out: ΔL* from white to #767676 is 50.4 for 4.54:1.
- **[CALC] Holding OKLCH L constant does not hold WCAG ratio constant across hues**, because WCAG uses Y with fixed coefficients and OKLab L does not include that weighting: at L = 0.70, C = 0.12 on #121212 the ratio ranges 6.61 (hue 0) to 7.42 (hue 180); L = 0.55, C = 0.15 on white ranges 4.54 (hue 150) to 5.27 (hue 0) across in-gamut hues. Spread is about 12 to 16% of the ratio, so hold L fixed to keep perceived lightness and hue-family consistent, then verify each accent's actual WCAG ratio (and optionally APCA Lc) per theme.
- **[CALC] HSL luminance spread at the same HSL lightness (hsl(h 100% 50%)):** WCAG relative luminance red 0.2126, yellow 0.9278, green 0.7152, cyan 0.7874, blue 0.0722, magenta 0.2848, a 13-fold range, which is why a hue-preserving light/dark flip is unreliable in HSL.
- A single-source pattern that works in Gecko 158: define `--accent: oklch(0.55 0.12 250)` for light and derive the dark variant via `oklch(from var(--accent) 0.75 c h)`, or use `light-dark(oklch(...), oklch(...))` (Firefox 120; requires a `color-scheme`). Because Thunderbird chrome and message content both render in Gecko, all these functions are expected to work in `userChrome.css` and `userContent.css`; I did not test it in Thunderbird itself.
- Chroma must be re-checked after lightening: some hue/L/C combinations fall outside sRGB (in the sweep above, hues near 60 to 120 and 180 to 240 were out of gamut at L 0.55, C 0.15), and CSS gamut-maps them, which can move lightness and therefore contrast. Choose C within gamut at the target L.

### Gaps
- Ottosson's comparison is against CAM16-UCS predictions, not against psychophysical data; I found no independent psychophysical head-to-head of OKLab versus CIELAB lightness uniformity for UI-sized colour differences in this session.
- Not tested: actual rendering of `oklch(from ...)` inside Thunderbird 158 `userChrome.css` / message-pane CSS (engine support per MDN BCD is the basis).
- Thunderbird version-to-Gecko mapping is from the installed Flatpak version string plus secondary news; the official Mozilla release-notes page for 158 was not retrieved.

## 6. Minimum size and thickness for coloured bars and dots to be perceptible

### Takeaway
No contrast standard sets a minimum size for icons or bars (WCAG 1.4.11 explicitly sets none). The vision-science evidence is that colour resolution is much coarser than luminance resolution, S-cone (blue-yellow) signals fail first for small foveal fields (foveal tritanopia, roughly 18 to 25 arcmin), and red-green sensitivity falls fastest in the periphery. Practically, a 2 to 4 px bar or a 5 to 8 px dot is below or near the size where hue is reliably conveyed, so it must be carried by lightness contrast plus a non-colour cue.

### Cited Findings
- **[ADVOCACY/guideline] APCA size floors:** Lc 15 non-semantic non-text "no less than 5px (solid) in its smallest dimension"; Lc 30 semantic "mostly solid" icons "Generally no less than 5.5px solid in its smallest dimension"; Lc 45 for fine-detail pictograms and outline icons; one reference px = 1.278 arc minutes. — [APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html). The WCAG 3 draft defines a CSS pixel as "visual angle of about 0.0213 degrees" (= 1.28 arcmin). — [WCAG 3.0 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/WD-wcag-3.0-20260910/)
- **[STD] WCAG 1.4.11:** no minimum size for graphical objects; thin lines "may be rendered ... with a much fainter color than the actual color"; advice to avoid particularly thin lines. — [Understanding SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)
- **[PEER] Chromatic spatial resolution is coarse (Mullen 1985, J Physiol 359:381-400):** "the limiting acuities based on red-green and blue-yellow colour discriminations are similar at 11 or 12 cycles/deg"; "Above 0.5 cycles/deg, contrast sensitivity is greater to monochromatic than to chromatic gratings"; chromatic contrast sensitivity is low-pass. — [PubMed 3999044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3999044/)
- **[PEER] Foveal tritanopia (Williams, MacLeod & Hayhoe 1981, Vision Res 21:1341-1356):** "an area roughly 25' in diameter in the central fovea lacks functioning blue-sensitive cones"; Willmer & Wright (1945), cited therein, found a centrally fixated 20' field matchable with only two primaries (tritanopic matches); the paper also shows scattered light can change the appearance of small central fields (a small fixated green field appears blue with a dim violet corona). — [Williams et al. 1981 PDF](https://aria.cvs.rochester.edu/papers/williams-etal_VR1981.pdf)
- **[PEER] B-cone acuity (Williams et al. 1981, Vision Res 21:1357-75, introduction):** reported blue-cone acuities of 2.9' (Stiles 1949), 3.75 to 5' (Daw & Enoch 1973), 7.5' (Brindley 1954); "even the most generous estimate of B cone acuity is roughly 6 times poorer than the resolution achieved by the R and G cones under optimal conditions"; two lights differing only in B-cone excitation fail "to form a distinct contour". — [Williams et al. 1981b PDF](https://aria.cvs.rochester.edu/papers/williams-etal_VR1981b.pdf)
- **[PEER] Anatomy (Curcio et al. 1991, J Comp Neurol 312:610-24):** blue cones are 7% of cones within 4 mm of the foveal centre and "foveal blue cones are sparse, irregularly spaced, and missing in a zone about 100 microns (0.35 degrees) in diameter near the site of peak cone density"; "Blue cone spacing may limit resolution of the blue channel out to 20-30 degrees eccentricity." — [PubMed 1722224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1722224/)
- **[PEER] Davies & Morland (2003), Br J Ophthalmol 87(6):742-6:** colour-matching with bipartite fields of 12' to 60' of arc gave "a median foveal tritanopic zone of 18' of arc" in 12 controls; some diabetic subjects up to 1 degree. — [PubMed 12770973](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12770973/)
- **[PEER] Small-field tritanopia, general (Volbrecht 2016, JOSA A 33(7):1226-35):** "If stimuli are made sufficiently small, color-normal individuals report a loss in hue perception, in particular a decrease in the perception of green, in both the fovea and peripheral retina"; the stimulus size at which it occurs decreases as retinal illuminance rises and is larger when rods contribute (no-bleach/mesopic). — [PubMed 27409678](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27409678/)
- **[PEER] Logvinenko (2001), Perception 30(2):223-32:** a similar colour illusion occurs with a large test field if its spatial-frequency content is high enough ("high-spatial-frequency tritanopia"), attributed to spatial filtering within colour channels rather than filling-in. — [PubMed 11296503](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11296503/)
- **[PEER] Periphery (Hansen, Pracejus & Gegenfurtner 2009, J Vision 9(4):26):** colour perception is best in the fovea; "Sensitivity to red-green color variations declines more steeply toward the periphery than sensitivity to luminance or blue-yellow colors"; chromatic detection worsens with eccentricity but remains possible to about 50 degrees with large enough stimuli. — [PubMed 19757935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19757935/)
- **Colour-blind observers and small objects (Okabe & Ito):** "Colorblind people find it especially difficult to distinguish the colors of thin lines and small symbols." — [Okabe & Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)

### Inferences
- **[CALC] Angular sizes (1 CSS px = 1.278 arcmin, reference viewing):** 2 px = 2.6', 3 px = 3.8', 4 px = 5.1', 6 px dot = 7.7', 8 px dot = 10.2', 12 px = 15.3'. A 2 px bar is about half of Mullen's 11 to 12 cycles/deg chromatic limit period (a 12 cpd period is 5', half-period 2.5'), so it sits at roughly the edge of red-green/blue-yellow chromatic resolution, and a 2 to 4 px bar (2.6' to 5.1') falls inside to below the 2.9' to 7.5' range quoted for blue-cone acuity and well under the 18' to 25' foveal tritanopic zone. Caveats: Mullen used extended sine gratings at fixed luminance, real displays at real viewing distances vary, and a bar or dot is not a grating; treat these as order-of-magnitude indicators, not thresholds.
- Hue-based distinctions that depend on S-cone signals (blue vs grey/green, yellow vs pink) are the least reliable at dot and bar sizes; L-M (red vs green) distinctions survive better for normal observers but fail first for deutan/protan observers and decline fastest in the periphery. A quote-level bar series that differs only in hue (e.g. blue / purple / teal) is therefore the weakest design; vary lightness and/or width/pattern as well.
- Because 1.4.11 says thin lines may render fainter than specified (anti-aliasing), the designer should aim for visible margin above 3:1 for 2 to 3 px bars (APCA's Lc 15 floor at 5 px solid is lower than WCAG's 3:1, so for bars under 5 px APCA's own guidance would not permit relying on them at all).

### Gaps
- No source gives a validated minimum width in px for a coloured bar or dot to keep its hue identifiable; the figures above are derived from foveal-tritanopia and chromatic-acuity studies, not from UI experiments.
- No guidance was found on colour appearance of thin lines specific to email quote bars.
- Viewing-distance and pixel-density assumptions (96 dpi CSS reference pixel vs high-dpi laptops) were not researched; the arithmetic uses the CSS reference-pixel definition only.

## 7. What the numbers imply for each element type (synthesis for the designer)

### Takeaway
Treat every accent as a pair of tuned variants (light, dark) that holds hue and shifts OKLCH lightness, verify with the WCAG ratio (the legal and tooling baseline) and an APCA check for the dark variant, and never let hue alone carry the meaning of a dot or bar.

### Cited Findings
- **Text accents (sender names, links, dates), body-sized:** WCAG AA 4.5:1 ([SC 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)); Apple asks 4.5:1 minimum and 7:1 "especially in small text" ([HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)); WCAG AAA 7:1 ([SC 1.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)). APCA: Lc 75 minimum for body columns, Lc 60 for non-body content text, add Lc 15 to use content-text sizes as body ([Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). Large text (24 px, or 18.5 px bold) 3:1.
- **Inline links:** colour alone is insufficient unless 3:1 against surrounding text per G183 ([SC 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)).
- **Small glyphs (unread dot, flag icon):** WCAG 1.4.11 needs 3:1 against adjacent colours if the glyph carries meaning without text ([SC 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)); APCA: Lc 30 for solid semantic icons at 5.5 px+, Lc 45 for fine-detail or outline icons ([Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)); Apple: pair colour with "distinct shapes or icons" ([HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)).
- **Thin bars (quote-level bars, 2 to 4 px):** WCAG gives 3:1 only if the bars are needed to understand content and warns of faint rendering; APCA treats non-semantic non-text at Lc 15 minimum and 5 px solid; vision science puts hue reliability for such widths in doubt (section 6).
- **Dark-mode variants:** Material: avoid saturated colours, use lighter desaturated tones (200-50 range) ([M2 dark theme](https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html)); Apple: separate bright and dim variants, "no lower than 4.5:1", strive for 7:1 ([HIG Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)).

### Inferences
- **[CALC] Starting lightness targets for neutral-ish accents (OKLCH L, chroma about 0.10 to 0.15, verify per hue):** light theme on white: about 0.55 for 4.5:1 (0.50 gives about 5.6 to 6.4), about 0.67 for 3:1 glyphs and bars, about 0.46 for 7:1. Dark theme on #121212 to #1e1e1e: about 0.59 for 4.5:1 and 0.49 for 3:1 by WCAG; but if APCA's polarity model is credited, text-size accents need about 0.72 to 0.78 (WCAG about 6.3 to 8.8, Lc about -49 to -65). Hue changes the WCAG ratio at fixed L by up to roughly 12 to 16% of the ratio (section 5), so check each hue.
- Bars and dots on dark backgrounds are the cheapest to over-deliver: a 3:1 target costs only OKLCH L about 0.49 to 0.50 on #121212, but APCA |Lc| 30 on dark corresponds to roughly WCAG 3.9:1 to 4.6:1 (greys #767676 to #7a7a7a on #000 to #1e1e1e), and CVD/size evidence argues for luminance margin beyond that. A reasonable design stance is 4.5:1 or better for thin bars and dots in dark mode, 3:1 minimum in light mode, with a second cue (width, glyph, position).
- Luminance-first selection plus a non-colour cue satisfies the overlap of WCAG 1.4.1/1.4.11, Okabe & Ito, Apple HIG, and the WCAG 3 draft's CVD caveat; the sources do not support relying on hue difference between adjacent accent colours (unread dot vs flag vs quote level) at these sizes.
